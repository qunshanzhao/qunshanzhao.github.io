My aim has always been to positively influence policy and practice in using new forms of urban big data and advanced quantitative methods to understand urban phenomena — and to use credible, persuasive evidence to improve the form and sustainability of the built environment.

<h2 id="heat" style="margin-top:2.2rem;scroll-margin-top:90px;">1 · Hot Homes — heat inequality and sensor-enhanced housing surveys (Southwark, London)</h2>

<p style="margin:4px 0 10px;">
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;font-weight:600;background:rgba(224,49,49,0.14);color:#e03131;">Partners: the Bureau of Investigative Journalism &amp; Impact on Urban Health</span>
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4);">2023–</span>
</p>

In **[Hot Homes](https://lambethclimatepartnership.org/case-study/hot-homes)** (August–September 2023), the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, Impact on Urban Health and our UBDC team fitted sensors in 40 South London homes, recording temperature, humidity and air quality every minute, alongside qualitative accounts of residents' health and wellbeing — developing and field-testing our **sensor-enhanced housing survey** methodology for monitoring indoor heat.

Headline findings:

- Despite a relatively mild summer, **every monitored home** exceeded the WHO-recommended 25°C indoor maximum for London — one stayed above it for over three weeks, including at night
- Some homes were **10°C hotter inside than outside** overnight
- During the one hot week, **80% of participants** said the heat at home was affecting their physical and mental health

**From data to policy — the Southwark influence chain:**

1. **Autumn 2023 · consultation timing.** The monitoring period coincided with Southwark's public consultation on its draft Climate Resilience and Adaptation Strategy (29 Aug – 9 Oct 2023); the council worked with the research team to consider the data, and officers met study participants on 13 September 2023 to hear residents' experiences first-hand
2. **Consultation report.** The report includes a dedicated "Hot homes study" section, and "hot homes" was listed among the key issues raised in the Cabinet report
3. **February 2024 · strategy adopted.** The Climate Resilience and Adaptation Strategy introduced **Action A7** — *"Develop an approach to tackling 'hot homes' so residents who are most vulnerable to this issue have cooler homes"* (jointly led by Asset Management and Public Health) — alongside doubling cool spaces by 2026, home-temperature monitoring via social care visits, overheating advice through the Green Homes Advice Service, and climate risk mapping, with **£1m allocated**
4. **September 2024 · annual report.** Southwark's Climate Change Annual Report explicitly credits the Bureau's *"landmark study"* as the driver of the hot homes action
5. **Spring 2025 · exemplar practice.** The LGA / London Councils *London Leading* series features Southwark's strategy development, noting the SIIP (2024–27) data programme supporting the hot-homes pilot
6. **May 2025 · public health evidence.** The Hot Weather &amp; Health JSNA uses Hot Homes as core evidence — 1 in 8 participants sought medical care as a direct result of their hot home; every participating household with a baby exceeded temperature thresholds linked to sudden infant death risk; communal-heating homes ran about 1°C hotter; and 25% of participants had *"nowhere to go"* for respite — justifying the expansion of cool spaces
7. **September 2025 · strategies merged.** Cabinet merged the 2021 Climate Change Strategy and the 2024 adaptation strategy so that adaptation carries equal weight with emissions reduction; the Hot Homes lineage continues in **Action 1** (a portfolio of measures for residents most vulnerable to overheating, embedded in housing asset management) and **Action 24** (an NHS/voluntary-sector programme built on the JSNA recommendations, plus expanded cool spaces coverage)

<h2 id="mobility" style="margin-top:2.4rem;scroll-margin-top:90px;">2 · Mobile phone app data for understanding travel behaviour — Glasgow City Council &amp; Glasgow City Region</h2>

<p style="margin:4px 0 10px;">
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;font-weight:600;background:rgba(25,113,194,0.14);color:#1971c2;">Partners: Glasgow City Council (GCC) &amp; Glasgow City Region (GCR)</span>
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4);">2020–</span>
</p>

As project co-lead of UBDC's mobile phone data research group, I helped turn two major mobile phone app datasets (Huq and Tamoco) into policy-ready mobility products — origin–destination (OD) matrices, home location estimation, and site visitation patterns — delivered to GCC and GCR for transport planning, greenspace management, and post-pandemic economic recovery, when traditional surveys were too slow to capture changing behaviour ([methodology paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apgeog.2023.102997)).

Where the products have been used:

- **Strategic Parking Plan** — Atkins used the OD matrices to model travel and parking demand, feeding into the Glasgow Transport Strategy Spatial Delivery Framework
- **Clyde Mission** — GCR analysed pre-/during-pandemic commuting to the Clyde River corridor
- **Open space use** — visitation patterns for 51 open spaces; in-depth analyses of Kelvingrove Park and Glasgow Green (visitor volumes, local vs non-local, daily profiles) supporting Parks Management budgeting and the Open Space Strategy
- **Transport strategy appraisal** — Jacobs/Steer combined the OD matrices with Census travel-to-work data to appraise interventions such as public transport corridors
- **Strategic Bus Network Plan** — Jacobs used the OD matrices to help design the bus network Glasgow needs
- **City Network modelling** — AECOM is using the OD matrices for full-city traffic modelling for the Liveable Neighbourhoods programme, with outputs owned by GCC for reuse
- **City of Edinburgh Council** — as long-standing council collaborators moved on to Edinburgh, mobility data products have now been shared with the City of Edinburgh Council to explore similar applications (2026, ongoing)

At Glasgow I brought the model to the Salvation Army UK: we co-designed an SGSSS collaborative PhD studentship (won in 2021, PhD student [Mohd Sarim]({{ '/people/#mohd-sarim' | relative_url }})) analysing internal data from their Employment Plus and community services to improve service efficiency — with a longer-term aim of extending data-driven decision-making across the Salvation Army's international branches and improving their data collection strategies.

**Sustrans (2021–).** Through an MSc collaborative dissertation (2021) and an MSc group project (2022) on primary and secondary school street interventions, our analyses were incorporated into Sustrans' work — including the active travel dashboard showing how pupils travel to school. Sustrans provided a paid internship for the dissertation student, and one of our 2022–23 MSc Urban Analytics graduates now holds a permanent position there.

<h2 id="spopt" style="margin-top:2.4rem;scroll-margin-top:90px;">4 · Open-source spatial optimisation in practice — from spopt to UCL student placements</h2>

<p style="margin:4px 0 10px;">
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;font-weight:600;background:rgba(8,127,91,0.16);color:#087f5b;">Partners: PySAL &amp; AGILE &amp; UCL</span>
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4);">2021–</span>
</p>

Through [PySAL/spopt](https://github.com/pysal/spopt) I contribute to and mentor open-source facility-location modelling — including four [Google Summer of Code projects]({{ '/opensource/' | relative_url }}) (2021–2025) and the **AGILE funding initiative** project *[Development of the spopt location modelling package to include the capacitated p-median problem](https://agile-gi.eu/activities/initiatives/development-of-open-source-python-modelling-package)* (2023, PI; [final report](https://agile-gi.eu/images/initiatives/documents/Funding_initiative_spopt_finalreport.pdf), [demo video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u9EOdfe82Gs), [workshop materials](https://github.com/ljwolf/intro-to-spatialopt)).

The capacitated location-allocation methods were then applied to a real operational problem: **matching UCL trainee teachers to school placements across London** while respecting school capacities and minimising public-transport journey times ([GISRUK 2025 update](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15231178) — Bearman, Gaboardi, Roddy, Zhao, Chen &amp; Wolf).

Impact so far (reported by our UCL collaborators, July 2026):

- UCL has built the method into an internal tool — **APA (Automated Placement Allocation)** — now under user testing by the UCL Institute of Education
- Primary placements that previously took **over a week** of manual work are now allocated in **about 25 minutes**
- Around **60% of trainee teachers receive better journeys** and a further 20% equivalent journeys, compared with manual allocation
- UCL plans to extend the tool to **Pharmacy placements** next, and other universities running educational placements have expressed interest in adopting it

<h3 style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Glasgow Low Emission Zone</h3>

<p style="margin:4px 0 10px;">
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;font-weight:600;background:rgba(95,61,196,0.14);color:#5f3dc4;">Policy: Glasgow City Council Low Emission Zone</span>
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4);">2024–</span>
</p>

Our study [*Did the implementation of the Low Emission Zone in Glasgow change the traffic flow and air quality?*](https://doi.org/10.32866/001c.123382) (Findings, 2024) provided one of the first independent evaluations of Glasgow's LEZ after enforcement began in June 2023, using the city's SCOOT traffic sensor network and air-quality monitoring data: normalised NO₂ fell by 25–35% on High Street and 9–13% on Hope Street while traffic volumes stayed broadly stable — early evidence that the LEZ discourages high-emission vehicles in the city centre. The findings were reported by **BBC Radio Scotland**, **Heart Radio** ([interview]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }})), **the Scotsman**, the Glasgow Chamber of Commerce, and Glasgow City of Science and Innovation, and are featured as a UBDC [impact story](https://www.ubdc.ac.uk/impact-story/air-quality-has-improved-in-low-emission-zones) (see also the project page [Glasgow Low Emission Zone Impact](https://www.ubdc.ac.uk/research-theme/city-modelling-sensing/low-emission-zone)). Glasgow City Council's own monitoring later corroborated this trajectory: the [2025 Air Quality Annual Progress Report](https://glasgow.gov.uk/article/13535/City-centre-air-pollution-drops-by-a-third-following-LEZ-enforcement), presented to the council's Net Zero and Climate Monitoring Committee in August 2025, recorded a 34% reduction in NO₂ within the LEZ against 2022 and the city's first-ever full compliance with statutory air-quality objectives. *(Case study in development.)*

<h3 style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Sensing Gorbals — community heat and air quality at the LEZ fringe</h3>

<p style="margin:4px 0 10px;">
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;font-weight:600;background:rgba(232,89,12,0.14);color:#e8590c;">Partner: Crossroads Youth &amp; Community Association</span>
  <span style="display:inline-block;padding:2px 10px;border-radius:1rem;font-size:0.8em;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4);">2025–</span>
</p>

**[Sensing Environmental Changes at the fringe of the new Glasgow LEZ](https://www.gla.ac.uk/research/az/sustainablesolutions/ourprojects/gallant/innovationfundprojects/sensingchangeheatandairqualityinthegorbals/)** (GALLANT Innovation Fund) is an ongoing community sensing campaign monitoring heat and air quality in the Gorbals — a neighbourhood sitting just outside the Low Emission Zone boundary — co-designed and co-funded with the [Crossroads Youth &amp; Community Association](https://www.cyca.org.uk/). So far the team ([Xingyi Du]({{ '/people/#xingyi-du' | relative_url }}) and [Dr Yunbei Ou]({{ '/people/#yunbei-ou' | relative_url }})) has installed **ten fixed sensors** around the neighbourhood — mounted mainly on the Gorbals' wooden bee posts — which measured continuously through the recent heat wave; run **two community workshops** with local residents; and launched a **portable sensing campaign** with Atmotube PRO 2 wearable monitors, with in-home sensors being prepared ahead of the next anticipated heat wave. The project connects the LEZ evaluation work above to the neighbourhood scale; see the UBDC project page [Environmental Change in Glasgow's Gorbals](https://www.ubdc.ac.uk/research-theme/city-modelling-sensing/environmental-change-in-glasgows-gorbals). *(Case study in development.)*
